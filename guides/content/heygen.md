## The one-liner

@body_lead HeyGen makes videos of people who were never filmed. Type a script, pick a presenter — a stock avatar or a digital twin of yourself made from two minutes of webcam footage — and out comes a talking-head video with natural gestures, lip-sync and, if you want, the same video in 175 other languages.

It is the presenter seat in a solo founder's stack, and for marketing and training teams it replaced the studio booking. In 2026 it added the agent: one prompt, and it writes, voices, shoots and edits the whole thing.

## What it actually does

@cap <b>Avatars</b> — Five hundred-plus stock presenters, or your own. Avatar IV and the newer Avatar V read the emotional register of the audio and generate matching expressions, head turns and hand gestures; the stiff mannequin of two years ago is gone.

@cap <b>Digital twins</b> — A custom avatar from two minutes of footage; a photo avatar from a single still. Your face, delivering scripts you never read aloud.

@cap <b>Video Agent</b> — The 2026 headline. One text prompt becomes a complete video: script, visuals, presenter, voice-over, edit. Under four minutes to a finished, postable piece. Speed and precision modes for quick clips versus high-stakes content.

@cap <b>Translation</b> — Upload a video; get it back in another language, in a matching voice, with the mouth movements re-synced. 175-plus languages, from about thirty source languages.

@cap <b>Interactive avatars</b> — A presenter that answers questions live, for sales pages, kiosks and support. A separate LiveAvatar track, billed by the real-time minute, with extra slots at $49 a month.

@cap <b>Brand kits, templates, SCORM</b> — The corporate plumbing: consistent styling, reusable layouts, exports that drop into learning systems.

@cap <b>API and MCP</b> — A standalone pay-as-you-go pool: buy the credits you want, no subscription, and they last twelve months. Studio video, translation and LiveAvatar, so other agents can make HeyGen videos on your behalf.

## The catches

- → <b>Credits, again.</b> The realistic avatars cost eight to twelve times the old ones per minute. Budget in finished minutes at the quality you will actually publish.
- → <b>The uncanny gap is small, not zero.</b> Avatar IV and V are good enough for corporate content and most marketing; they are not a substitute for a founder on camera when trust is the product.
- → <b>Consent is lighter than at rivals.</b> Security researchers have noted HeyGen's avatar-creation consent checks are less strict than Synthesia's. Cloning anyone but yourself needs their written permission; the platform's leniency is not your defence.
- → <b>Disclosure rules are arriving.</b> Several jurisdictions and platforms now require labelling synthetic presenters in ads and political content. Label where required.
- → <b>Your twin lives there.</b> A digital twin is an asset on HeyGen's servers; extra slots cost $29 a month each. Export finished videos; the avatar does not travel.

## Who it is for

@fit <b>Yes</b> — Anyone producing structured, script-driven video at volume: explainers, product demos, course modules, sales outreach, multilingual versions of the same thing. Creator at $29 if it is a few videos a month; Pro if it is a few a week.

@fit <b>Not yet</b> — Anyone whose audience needs to trust a real face, or who is unsure of their monthly volume. Film ten videos yourself first, then decide.

@fit <b>No</b> — Anyone who needs cinematic or generated footage rather than a presenter (Higgsfield, Runway), or only needs the voice (ElevenLabs).

## Why it matters

HeyGen is what "AI-run company" looks like at the point of contact with a customer: a presenter that does not exist explaining a product that an agent built, in whatever language the viewer speaks, produced from one prompt in four minutes. The economics of video content changed in 2026, and this is the tool that changed them for the people who make it.

@gl <b>Credits</b> — The usage currency. Rates vary by feature and look: 4 a minute for an Avatar III video look, 31 for Avatar IV, 48 for Avatar V, 40 for the Video Agent.

@gl <b>Interactive avatar</b> — A presenter that responds live to questions. Enterprise, per slot.

@gl <b>SCORM</b> — The export format learning-management systems accept; a Business-plan feature.

@gl <b>Consent</b> — Documented permission from anyone whose likeness you clone. Required regardless of what the platform enforces.
